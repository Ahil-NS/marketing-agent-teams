# Wellness Content Compliance Rules

Critical compliance rules for generating wellness, health, and mindfulness content. All agents producing wellness vertical content MUST follow these rules to avoid legal liability, platform penalties, and audience harm.

---

## Core Compliance Principles

### 1. No Medical Claims
- **NEVER** claim that any product, practice, or routine cures, treats, diagnoses, or prevents any disease or medical condition
- **NEVER** provide specific medical advice, dosage recommendations, or treatment protocols
- **NEVER** suggest replacing professional medical care with wellness practices
- **ALWAYS** distinguish between general wellness tips and medical advice

### 2. Approved Language Patterns
Use soft, experiential language that avoids absolute health claims:

**APPROVED phrases:**
- "may help support..."
- "some people find that..."
- "research suggests..."
- "could contribute to..."
- "has been associated with..."
- "many wellness practitioners recommend..."
- "talk to your healthcare provider about..."
- "consider incorporating..."
- "you might try..."

**BANNED phrases:**
- "cures" / "heals" / "treats" / "fixes"
- "clinically proven to..."
- "guaranteed to..."
- "eliminates [condition]"
- "you should take [supplement]"
- "this will make you..."
- "doctors don't want you to know..."
- "Big Pharma doesn't want you to see this..."
- "alternative to [medication]"
- "natural remedy for [disease]"
- "detox" (when implying toxin removal — the body's liver and kidneys handle detoxification)

### 3. Required Disclaimers
Include appropriate disclaimers based on content type:

**General wellness content:**
> "This content is for informational purposes only and is not intended as medical advice. Consult your healthcare provider before making changes to your health routine."

**Exercise content:**
> "Consult your physician before beginning any exercise program. Listen to your body and modify movements as needed."

**Nutrition content:**
> "Nutritional needs vary by individual. This content is not a substitute for professional dietary advice."

**Mental health content:**
> "If you or someone you know is in crisis, contact the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741)."

## Platform-Specific Health Content Policies

### TikTok
- **Restricts unverified health claims** — content may be suppressed or removed
- **Bans content promoting:** dangerous weight loss methods, eating disorders, self-harm
- **Medical misinformation policy:** Claims contradicting scientific consensus are removed
- **Supplement restrictions:** Cannot promote supplements with health claims
- **Strategy:** Keep health content educational, cite sources, use approved language

### Reddit
- **Community-specific rules:** Many health subreddits (r/health, r/fitness, r/mentalhealth) have strict rules against medical advice
- **Auto-moderation:** Certain health keywords trigger automatic moderation review
- **Ban risk:** Providing medical advice can result in permanent bans from health communities
- **Strategy:** Frame content as personal experience, link to professional resources, respect community rules

### Facebook / Instagram (Meta)
- **Health misinformation policy:** Content rated false by fact-checkers gets reduced distribution
- **Vaccine and health claim moderation:** Stricter since 2021
- **Eating disorder content:** Instagram restricts content promoting unhealthy body image
- **Supplement advertising:** Must comply with FTC guidelines for sponsored content
- **Strategy:** Use evidence-based language, cite reputable sources, avoid sensationalism

## Regulatory Compliance

### FTC (Federal Trade Commission) — United States
- **Endorsements:** Must disclose material connections (sponsorship, free products)
- **Health claims:** Must be substantiated by reliable scientific evidence
- **Testimonials:** Cannot imply typical results unless substantiated
- **Influencer guidelines:** Clear and conspicuous disclosure (#ad, #sponsored)

### FDA (Food and Drug Administration) — United States
- **Supplement claims:** Structure/function claims allowed, disease claims prohibited
- **Required disclaimer for supplements:** "These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."
- **Cosmetic claims:** Cannot claim therapeutic benefit
- **"Natural" labeling:** No formal FDA definition — use carefully

### HIPAA Considerations
- **Never share** identifiable health information about specific individuals
- User-generated health stories should be used only with explicit consent
- Aggregate data only — never identify individuals' health conditions

## Sensitive Topic Guidelines

### Mental Health Content
- Always include crisis resources when discussing suicide, self-harm, or crisis situations
- Avoid graphic descriptions of self-harm methods or suicide attempts
- Never minimize mental health conditions or suggest they're "all in your head"
- Frame therapy and professional help positively — reduce stigma
- Avoid creating content that could trigger vulnerable individuals
- Use content warnings when discussing potentially triggering topics

### Eating Disorders
- Never promote specific diets, calorie counting, or extreme restriction
- Avoid "before/after" body comparisons
- Focus on body neutrality over body positivity when possible
- Do not use language that moralizes food ("clean eating," "cheat meals," "guilty pleasure")
- Include NEDA (National Eating Disorders Association) helpline when relevant: (800) 931-2237

### Addiction & Recovery
- Never glamorize substance use or addictive behaviors
- Respect anonymity — never identify individuals in recovery without consent
- Use person-first language: "person with a substance use disorder" not "addict"
- Include SAMHSA National Helpline when relevant: 1-800-662-4357
- Frame recovery as a journey, not a destination

### Chronic Illness
- Never suggest that wellness practices can replace medical treatment
- Acknowledge the real challenges of living with chronic conditions
- Avoid toxic positivity ("just think positive and you'll get better")
- Center the patient's experience and autonomy
- Respect the complexity of chronic illness management

## Citation and Source Requirements

### When to Cite Sources
- Any statistic or numerical claim ("60% of adults...")
- Any reference to research studies or scientific findings
- Health benefit claims, even with soft language
- Claims about nutrients, supplements, or foods

### Approved Source Types
- Peer-reviewed medical journals (JAMA, The Lancet, BMJ, NEJM)
- Government health agencies (CDC, NIH, WHO, FDA)
- University medical centers and research institutions
- Professional medical associations (AMA, APA, ACSM)

### Source Types to AVOID
- Wellness blogs with no clinical backing
- Social media influencers (as primary source)
- Single studies without replication
- Retracted studies
- "Ancient wisdom" claims without modern evidence

---

## Content Review Checklist

Before publishing wellness content, verify:

1. [ ] No absolute health claims (cure, treat, diagnose, prevent)
2. [ ] Approved language patterns used throughout
3. [ ] Required disclaimers included based on content type
4. [ ] Platform-specific policies respected
5. [ ] Crisis resources included for mental health content
6. [ ] Sources cited for statistics and research references
7. [ ] No identifiable health information shared
8. [ ] Sensitive topics handled with appropriate warnings
9. [ ] FTC disclosure included for sponsored/partnered content
10. [ ] Content reviewed for potential harm to vulnerable audiences

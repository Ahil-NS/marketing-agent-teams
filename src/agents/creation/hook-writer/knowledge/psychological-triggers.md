# Psychological Triggers for Hook Writing

## Trigger Taxonomy

### 1. Curiosity Gaps
**Definition**: Creating an information deficit that the reader feels compelled to close.

**How it works**: The brain treats unanswered questions as "open loops" — and naturally seeks closure. A curiosity gap hook presents enough information to intrigue but withholds the resolution.

**Patterns**:
- "The one thing most people get wrong about [topic]..."
- "I discovered something about [topic] that changed everything"
- "You're probably making this mistake with [activity] and don't even know it"
- "What happens when you [unexpected action] for 30 days"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- Reddit: Works best with "here's the data" follow-through (don't leave the gap unfilled)
- TikTok: "Wait for it" format — gap is filled by the end of the video
- Facebook: Question format that invites comments to fill the gap together
- Instagram: Carousel "swipe to reveal" format is ideal for curiosity gaps

**Ethical boundary**: The payoff must match the promise. Never create a curiosity gap you don't deliver on.

---

### 2. Social Proof
**Definition**: Demonstrating that others are already engaging with, benefiting from, or endorsing something.

**How it works**: Humans are social creatures — we look to others' behavior to validate our own choices. Social proof reduces perceived risk and increases trust.

**Patterns**:
- "Over [number] people have already [action]"
- "The [method] that [credible group] swears by"
- "I asked 500 [relevant people] and here's what they said"
- "Why [industry leaders] are all switching to [thing]"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- Reddit: Community endorsement ("this is what worked for the community")
- TikTok: Trending sounds/formats as implicit social proof
- Facebook: Comments and shares as visible social proof signals
- Instagram: User-generated content, testimonials, "as seen in"

**Ethical boundary**: All numbers must be real. Never fabricate social proof metrics.

---

### 3. Urgency / Scarcity
**Definition**: Creating a time-limited or quantity-limited frame that motivates immediate action.

**How it works**: The perception of scarcity increases perceived value. Time pressure triggers loss aversion — we'd rather act now than risk missing out.

**Patterns**:
- "Before this [opportunity] disappears..."
- "This won't last — here's why you need to act now"
- "Only available until [date/condition]"
- "The window for [opportunity] is closing fast"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- TikTok: "Trending NOW" — ride the wave before it passes
- Instagram: Stories with countdown stickers, limited-time offers
- Facebook: Event-based urgency (seasonal, launch, deadline)
- Reddit: Less effective — Reddit audiences are skeptical of artificial urgency

**Ethical boundary**: Urgency must be REAL. Never create false scarcity or fake deadlines. The audience will discover and punish manufactured urgency.

---

### 4. Authority / Expertise
**Definition**: Establishing credibility through credentials, data, experience, or association with trusted sources.

**How it works**: People defer to perceived experts — authority reduces the cognitive effort needed to evaluate information.

**Patterns**:
- "As a [credential/experience], here's what I've learned about [topic]"
- "The research is clear: [evidence-based claim]"
- "[X years] of experience taught me this one thing about [topic]"
- "According to [credible source], [surprising finding]"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- Reddit: Heavily valued — "I'm a [professional] with [X years] experience" format
- TikTok: "Things I learned as a [profession]" format
- Facebook: Link to research or cite experts for share-worthy authority
- Instagram: Infographic carousels with cited data are highly save-worthy

**Ethical boundary**: Never fabricate credentials. Always cite real sources. Authority must be earned, not claimed.

---

### 5. Loss Aversion
**Definition**: Framing the message around what the reader will LOSE by not acting, rather than what they'll gain.

**How it works**: Psychological research shows losses are felt ~2x more powerfully than equivalent gains. "Don't miss out" is stronger than "you could gain."

**Patterns**:
- "You're losing [value] every day you don't [action]"
- "The mistake that's costing you [specific amount/outcome]"
- "Stop wasting [resource] on [ineffective thing]"
- "What [not doing X] is really costing you"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- Facebook: "Don't make this mistake" format drives protective sharing
- Instagram: Warning-style posts with save-worthy corrective advice
- TikTok: "Warning" or "Stop doing this" format — strong pattern interrupt
- Reddit: "[PSA]" format warning about common mistakes

**Ethical boundary**: The loss must be real and proportionate. Don't exaggerate risks to manipulate.

---

### 6. Identity / Belonging
**Definition**: Connecting the content to who the reader IS or aspires to be, creating in-group recognition.

**How it works**: People are drawn to content that reflects, validates, or elevates their identity. "This is for people like me" is a powerful filter.

**Patterns**:
- "If you're a [identity], you need to know this"
- "Things only [group] will understand"
- "For my [identity] people — this one's for you"
- "You might be a [identity] if [relatable behavior]"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- TikTok: "POV: you're a [identity]" — highly viral identity hooks
- Instagram: Aesthetic/lifestyle identity alignment
- Reddit: Subreddit-specific identity (community membership IS identity)
- Facebook: Group-based identity hooks ("Fellow [group members]...")

**Ethical boundary**: Never use identity hooks that exclude, demean, or stereotype. Identity hooks should celebrate, not gate-keep.

---

### 7. Novelty
**Definition**: Presenting something new, surprising, counterintuitive, or previously unknown.

**How it works**: The brain's dopamine system is activated by novelty — unexpected information gets attention precisely because it doesn't match existing mental models.

**Patterns**:
- "I just discovered something about [topic] that nobody talks about"
- "This changes everything about how we think about [topic]"
- "Set aside everything you know about [topic] — here's the truth"
- "The counterintuitive approach to [goal] that actually works"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- Reddit: "TIL" (Today I Learned) format — novelty is Reddit's currency
- TikTok: "Things you didn't know about [topic]" — discovery content
- Facebook: Share-worthy "mind-blown" moments
- Instagram: "Did you know?" carousel format

**Ethical boundary**: Novelty must be genuine. Don't repackage common knowledge as "shocking."

---

### 8. FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)
**Definition**: Creating a sense that the reader is missing out on something valuable that others are already experiencing.

**How it works**: FOMO leverages social comparison and loss aversion — when people believe others are benefiting from something they're not part of, they feel compelled to act. It combines urgency with social proof to create immediate motivation.

**Patterns**:
- "Everyone is already doing [thing] — here's how to catch up"
- "You're the last person to know about [valuable thing]"
- "This [trend/technique] is blowing up and you're missing it"
- "While you're still [old way], others are already [new way]"

**Platform-appropriate usage**:
- TikTok: "Why is nobody talking about this?" and trend-riding formats — FOMO drives completion
- Instagram: Stories with countdown timers, limited drops, "save before it's gone"
- Facebook: Event-based FOMO ("Everyone's going — are you?"), group exclusivity
- Reddit: Less effective — Reddit audiences are skeptical of manufactured FOMO

**Ethical boundary**: FOMO must reference genuine opportunities or trends. Never fabricate exclusivity or manufacture artificial scarcity to pressure action.

---

## Trigger Combinations

The most effective hooks often combine 2 triggers:

| Primary | Secondary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Authority | "A Harvard study found something unexpected about [topic]" |
| Social Proof | Urgency | "10,000 people switched this week — here's why" |
| Loss Aversion | Novelty | "The hidden cost of [common habit] nobody warned you about" |
| Identity | Curiosity | "If you're a [identity], you've probably been wondering about..." |
| Authority | Novelty | "After 10 years in [field], I just learned something that changes everything" |

**Rule**: Use at most 2 triggers per hook. More than 2 creates cognitive overload and feels manipulative.

---

## Ethical Boundaries — Non-Negotiable

1. **No deception**: Every claim in a hook must be deliverable in the content
2. **No fear-mongering**: Loss aversion hooks must address real risks, not manufactured fears
3. **No false scarcity**: Urgency must reflect genuine time/quantity limits
4. **No fabricated credentials**: Authority must be earned and verifiable
5. **No exploitation**: Never use emotional hooks that exploit vulnerable populations
6. **No engagement bait**: Hooks that exist solely to game metrics without delivering value are prohibited
7. **Brand alignment**: Every hook must pass the "would this brand say this?" test
8. **Platform respect**: Follow each platform's community norms — what works on TikTok may be inappropriate on Reddit

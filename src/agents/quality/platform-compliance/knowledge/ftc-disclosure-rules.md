# FTC Disclosure Rules — Compliance Knowledge Base

## Overview

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires that endorsements and testimonials in advertising reflect honest opinions of the endorser. When there is a "material connection" between an endorser and the marketer, that connection must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed.

## Key Regulations

### 16 CFR Part 255 — Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising

- Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser.
- If there is a connection between the endorser and the marketer that consumers would not expect, it must be disclosed.
- The disclosure must be clear and conspicuous.

### Material Connections That Require Disclosure

- **Payment**: Any monetary compensation for creating content
- **Free products**: Products or services received for free or at a discount
- **Employment**: Content creator is an employee of the brand
- **Family/personal relationships**: Creator has a personal relationship with brand representatives
- **Affiliate relationships**: Creator earns commission from purchases made through their links
- **Contests/sweepstakes**: Content created as part of a brand-sponsored contest
- **Gifted items**: Products sent unsolicited with the expectation of coverage

## Social Media Disclosure Formatting Rules

### General Requirements
- Disclosures must be **before** the "more" or "see more" fold — not buried
- Use simple, unambiguous language: `#ad`, `#sponsored`, `Paid partnership with [brand]`
- Disclosures must be in the **same language** as the content
- Disclosures must be **visible without additional action** (no clicking, scrolling, tapping)

### Insufficient Disclosures (Do NOT Accept)
- `#sp` or `#spon` (too abbreviated)
- `#ambassador` or `#partner` alone (ambiguous)
- Buried in a wall of hashtags at the end
- Only in a "Thank you to [brand]" without stating it's paid
- Disclosure only in profile bio, not in the post itself

### Platform-Specific Formatting

#### Instagram
- Use Instagram's "Paid partnership with [brand]" tag
- `#ad` must appear in the first line of the caption (above the fold)
- Stories: disclosure must appear on every frame
- Reels: `#ad` in caption + verbal mention recommended

#### TikTok
- Use TikTok's branded content toggle
- `#ad` in the caption (visible before "more")
- Verbal disclosure in the first 3 seconds recommended
- Duets/stitches: original and response both need disclosures

#### Reddit
- Title must include `[Sponsored]` or `[Ad]` prefix
- Body must clearly state the relationship with the brand
- Comply with subreddit-specific promotional content rules
- Do not disguise paid content as organic discussion

#### Facebook
- Use Facebook's Branded Content Tool (handshake icon)
- "Paid partnership with [brand]" header is required for sponsored content
- Group posts: additional subreddit-specific rules may apply
- Ads: "Sponsored" label is automatically applied by the platform

## FTC Enforcement Priorities

- Health and safety claims (highest priority)
- Financial products and services
- Products targeting children or vulnerable populations
- Claims of scientific backing without evidence
- Influencer marketing at scale (patterns of non-disclosure)

## Red Flags for Non-Compliance

1. **No disclosure at all** — any sponsored content without clear indication of paid relationship
2. **Hidden disclosure** — disclosure buried below fold, in hashtag soup, or in bio only
3. **Ambiguous language** — using `#partner` or `#collab` instead of explicit `#ad` or `#sponsored`
4. **Inconsistent disclosure** — some posts disclosed, others not, for the same campaign
5. **Misleading context** — content that appears to be organic personal recommendation when it's paid

# Claim Verification Guide

## Types of Claims to Check

### Statistics
- Specific numbers: "90% of users", "3x faster", "$2.5 billion market"
- Growth figures: "increased by 40%", "doubled since 2020"
- Survey results: "8 out of 10 professionals prefer..."

### Quotes
- Direct quotes attributed to individuals
- Claims about what someone said or published
- References to published statements

### Historical Facts
- Founding dates: "Founded in 1998"
- Historical events referenced in content
- Timeline claims

### Scientific Claims
- Health benefits: "meditation reduces cortisol by 25%"
- Research findings: "studies show..."
- Cause-and-effect assertions

### Comparative Claims
- Product comparisons: "faster than competitor X"
- Market position: "the #1 platform for..."
- Pricing comparisons

## Types of Claims to SKIP

### Opinions (Not Verifiable)
- "We believe in empowering creators"
- "Our approach is unique"
- Personal preferences and recommendations

### Marketing Superlatives (Not Factual)
- "Best-in-class solution"
- "Revolutionary platform"
- "World-class support"
- "Leading" (without market data)

### Subjective Assessments
- "Easy to use"
- "Beautiful design"
- "Incredible results"

## Verification Methodology

### Source Hierarchy (Most to Least Authoritative)
1. **Primary sources**: Original research, official government data, company SEC filings
2. **Peer-reviewed publications**: Academic journals, meta-analyses
3. **Government/institutional data**: Bureau of Labor Statistics, WHO, FDA
4. **Major news outlets**: Multiple reputable outlets reporting the same data
5. **Industry reports**: Gartner, Forrester, IDC, Statista
6. **Expert commentary**: Named experts with relevant credentials

### Cross-Reference Rules
- Never verify a claim from a single source alone when possible
- Always check the date of the source — data older than 3 years should be flagged
- Look for the ORIGINAL source, not articles citing secondary sources
- Check if the statistic is being used in the correct context

## Confidence Scoring Rubric

| Score Range | Description | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 90-100 | Verified | Multiple authoritative sources confirm; data is recent and contextually accurate |
| 70-89 | Likely Accurate | Single authoritative source confirms; no contradicting evidence found |
| 50-69 | Indirect Evidence | Related data supports the claim; exact verification not possible |
| 30-49 | Plausible but Unverified | Claim sounds reasonable but no supporting evidence found |
| 0-29 | Contradicted | Authoritative sources contradict the claim or no evidence exists |

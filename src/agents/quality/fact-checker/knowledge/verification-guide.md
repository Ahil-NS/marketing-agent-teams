# Fact-Checking Guidelines

## Verification Priority
1. **Health and safety claims**: Highest priority — can cause harm
2. **Statistical claims**: Numbers are easy to get wrong
3. **Competitor comparisons**: Legal risk if inaccurate
4. **Industry claims**: Credibility risk if wrong
5. **General facts**: Lower priority but still verify

## Source Quality Hierarchy
1. Primary sources (original research, official data)
2. Peer-reviewed publications
3. Government and institutional data
4. Major news outlets (multiple sources)
5. Industry reports and analyses
6. Expert opinions and commentary

## Red Flags
- Statistics without sources
- "Studies show..." without specific citation
- Superlatives ("best", "fastest", "only") without evidence
- Outdated statistics presented as current
- Cherry-picked data that misrepresents trends
- Comparisons without context

## Verification Steps
1. Identify the claim
2. Find original source
3. Verify source credibility
4. Check data freshness
5. Look for contradicting evidence
6. Assess framing and context

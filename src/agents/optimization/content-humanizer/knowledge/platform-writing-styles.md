# Platform Writing Styles

Per-platform native writing conventions for content humanization. Each platform has distinct communication norms that AI-generated content must match.

## Reddit

### Voice & Tone
- Casual, first-person, conversational
- Community-aware (reference subreddit culture when relevant)
- Self-deprecating humor is welcome
- Authenticity over polish — typos are more trustworthy than perfection

### Formatting
- Use Reddit markdown: **bold**, *italic*, ~~strikethrough~~
- Bullet lists for comparisons or tips
- "Edit:" or "Edit: grammar" at the bottom is a natural Reddit artifact
- TL;DR at the end of longer posts
- Paragraph breaks between thoughts (Reddit formatting requires double newline)

### Vocabulary
- "IMO", "IMHO", "tbh", "ngl"
- "This." as a one-word agreement
- "Can confirm" / "Can vouch for this"
- Reference to specific subreddits: "r/meditation has some great guides"
- "Source: personal experience" or "Source: tried it myself"

### What to Avoid
- Sales language or CTAs ("Buy now!", "Click here!")
- Corporate speak ("We're excited to announce...")
- Overly polished or formal prose
- Hashtags (Reddit doesn't use them)

### Example
AI: "Meditation offers numerous benefits for productivity and mental well-being. Many professionals have found that incorporating a daily practice can significantly improve focus and reduce stress levels."

Human (Reddit): "Okay so I've been meditating for about 3 months now and ngl it's been a game changer for my focus at work. I was super skeptical at first but honestly the difference is wild. Anyone else notice this?"

## TikTok

### Voice & Tone
- Punchy, high-energy, Gen-Z vernacular
- Hook-first — grab attention in the first line
- Emoji use is expected but not excessive
- Storytelling structure: hook → conflict → resolution → CTA
- Conversational and direct

### Formatting
- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences max)
- Line breaks for pacing
- Strategic emoji placement (not every sentence)
- Hashtags at the end (3-5 relevant ones)
- No formal paragraph structure

### Vocabulary
- "no cap", "lowkey", "fr", "iykyk"
- "POV:", "Wait for it", "This changed everything"
- "Storytime:", "Hot take:"
- Exclamation energy but not corporate excitement
- "bestie", "girlie", "y'all" (context-dependent)

### What to Avoid
- Long paragraphs
- Formal or academic tone
- Generic hashtags (#fyp, #foryou, #viral)
- Dense information without visual breaks

### Example
AI: "Incorporating a daily meditation practice into your morning routine can lead to significant improvements in focus, productivity, and overall mental health."

Human (TikTok): "okay but why did nobody tell me that meditating for 10 min every morning would literally fix my brain?? 🧘‍♀️

like my focus went from 📉 to 📈 in TWO WEEKS

not sponsored just genuinely obsessed"

## Facebook

### Voice & Tone
- Conversational, question-based engagement
- Slightly more mature audience tone than TikTok
- Personal storytelling works well
- Warm and relatable
- Community-building language

### Formatting
- Moderate emoji use (1-3 per post)
- Question hooks to drive comments
- Shorter posts perform better (under 80 characters for highest engagement)
- Line breaks for readability
- 1-2 hashtags maximum (or none)

### Vocabulary
- "Who else...?", "Am I the only one who...?"
- "Drop a 🙌 if you agree"
- Personal pronouns: "I", "we", "you"
- Conversational fillers: "honestly", "seriously", "can we talk about"

### What to Avoid
- Wall of text
- Too many hashtags
- Overly promotional tone without personal angle
- Generic corporate announcements without human touch

### Example
AI: "Research demonstrates that meditation can improve cognitive function and reduce stress. Studies show that regular practitioners experience better focus and emotional regulation."

Human (Facebook): "Honestly I started meditating like 3 months ago just to see what the hype was about... and now I can't imagine my mornings without it 😅

Anyone else get into it recently? What's been your experience?"

## Instagram

### Voice & Tone
- Visual-first language (reference the image/video)
- Lifestyle-oriented and aspirational but authentic
- Strategic line breaks for readability
- Natural call-to-action
- Mix of inspiration and practicality

### Formatting
- First 125 characters are critical (before "...more" truncation)
- Strategic line breaks with dots or dashes as separators
- Hashtags: 3-5 relevant ones (at bottom or in first comment)
- Emojis as bullet points or section markers
- Call-to-action feels organic, not forced

### Vocabulary
- "Link in bio" (only when applicable)
- "Save this for later 📌"
- "Double tap if you agree ❤️"
- Lifestyle language: "morning routine", "self-care", "glow up"
- Aspirational but grounded

### What to Avoid
- Starting with generic openers
- Overly long captions without visual breaks
- Too many hashtags (the 30-hashtag era is over)
- Purely informational without personal touch
- All caps (except single words for emphasis)

### Example
AI: "Meditation is a powerful tool for improving mental health and productivity. By dedicating just 10 minutes each morning to mindfulness practice, you can experience significant improvements in your daily focus and stress management."

Human (Instagram): "my 5am meditation setup ☁️
.
.
three months into daily practice and I finally get why everyone's obsessed with this

the focus boost alone has been worth it — but the real surprise? how much calmer I am when things go sideways at work 🧘‍♀️

started with just 5 min and worked up. no fancy app needed.

what's your go-to morning ritual? 👇"

# Brand Voice Application Guidelines

## Applying Tone in Campaign Plans

### Tone Categories
- **Professional:** Authority-driven, data-backed claims, industry terminology
- **Casual:** Conversational, approachable, uses contractions and colloquialisms
- **Inspirational:** Aspirational language, vision-casting, empowerment focus
- **Educational:** Clear explanations, step-by-step, jargon-free
- **Witty:** Clever wordplay, pop culture references, self-aware humor

### Tone Application Rules
1. Campaign names should reflect the brand tone (e.g., professional: "Q2 Growth Initiative"; casual: "Level Up Your Game")
2. Content theme descriptions should use vocabulary consistent with brand tone
3. Success metric descriptions should maintain professional clarity regardless of overall tone
4. Platform-specific adaptations may slightly shift tone but must not contradict brand voice

## Audience-Aware Messaging

### Audience Segment Adaptation
- Define primary and secondary audience segments
- Map messaging depth to audience expertise level
- Adjust vocabulary complexity per audience segment
- Consider audience pain points when crafting theme rationale

### Persona-Based Content Mapping
- Each content theme should resonate with at least one defined audience persona
- Themes targeting niche personas should have higher engagement expectations
- Cross-persona themes should be broader but still specific enough to feel relevant

## Messaging Constraints

### Brand Principles Integration
- Every campaign objective should align with at least one brand principle
- Content theme rationale should reference how the theme supports brand values
- Success metrics should measure principle alignment (brand sentiment, trust scores)

### Banned Phrases and Terminology
- Scan all generated text for banned phrases before output
- Suggest approved alternatives when banned terms would naturally appear
- Consider banned phrases in context — some may be acceptable in quotes or competitive analysis

## Platform-Specific Voice Adaptation

### Reddit
- More authentic, community-first tone
- Value-driven: lead with useful information, not promotion
- Use subreddit-specific language and norms
- Avoid corporate-speak; emphasize genuine expertise

### TikTok
- High energy, trend-aware language
- Short, punchy hooks that match platform cadence
- Informal, personality-driven
- Use trending audio/sound references in descriptions

### Facebook
- Community-oriented, conversation-starting
- Slightly more formal than TikTok but warmer than LinkedIn
- Story-driven: personal anecdotes, behind-the-scenes
- Encourage meaningful comments over passive reactions

### Instagram
- Visual-first language: describe imagery, aesthetic, mood
- Aspirational but attainable
- Hashtag-friendly: incorporate relevant hashtags naturally
- Caption style: micro-story format with CTA

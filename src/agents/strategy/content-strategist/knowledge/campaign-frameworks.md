# Campaign Planning Frameworks

## AIDA Model (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)

### Attention Phase
- Hook-based content: questions, bold statements, surprising data
- Platform-specific hooks: TikTok (visual + sound), Reddit (intriguing titles), Instagram (stunning visuals), Facebook (emotional stories)
- Content types: short-form video, eye-catching carousels, controversy/debate posts

### Interest Phase
- Educational deep-dives: how-tos, tutorials, explainers
- Storytelling: case studies, behind-the-scenes, day-in-the-life
- Community engagement: polls, Q&A sessions, discussion threads

### Desire Phase
- Social proof: testimonials, user-generated content, transformation stories
- Comparison content: before/after, competitive analyses, feature showcases
- Aspirational content: lifestyle imagery, success stories, vision-casting

### Action Phase
- Clear CTAs: sign-up, download, purchase, join community
- Limited-time offers, exclusive access, early-bird pricing
- Frictionless conversion paths: link in bio, swipe-up, comment keywords

## Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

### Understanding Customer Jobs
- **Functional jobs:** What task is the customer trying to accomplish?
- **Emotional jobs:** How does the customer want to feel?
- **Social jobs:** How does the customer want to be perceived?

### Mapping Content to Jobs
- Create content that helps customers accomplish their primary job
- Address pain points in the current solution landscape
- Show how your product/service makes the job easier, faster, or better

### Content Theme Derivation
- Each content theme should map to a specific customer job
- Themes should address the full job lifecycle: awareness of need → evaluation → execution → monitoring

## Positioning Framework

### Competitive Positioning
- Identify your unique value proposition relative to competitors
- Map competitive gaps where you have advantage
- Create content themes that reinforce your differentiated position

### Category Design
- If creating a new category: educate the market first
- Content themes should establish category vocabulary and standards
- Position thought leadership content to own the category narrative

## Content Marketing Funnel Stages

### Top of Funnel (Awareness)
- Trend-riding content, viral formats, educational content
- Goal: maximize reach and brand recognition
- Metrics: impressions, reach, video views, new followers

### Middle of Funnel (Consideration)
- How-to guides, comparisons, case studies, webinars
- Goal: establish authority and build trust
- Metrics: engagement rate, saves, shares, time spent

### Bottom of Funnel (Conversion)
- Testimonials, product demos, special offers, urgency-driven content
- Goal: drive specific actions (sign-up, purchase, download)
- Metrics: click-through rate, conversion rate, cost per acquisition

### Retention/Advocacy
- Community content, tips and tricks, exclusive insights, user spotlights
- Goal: reduce churn, increase lifetime value, generate referrals
- Metrics: retention rate, NPS, referral rate, repeat engagement

## Content Marketing Strategy Patterns

### The Content Pillar Model
- Define 3-5 core pillars that support brand authority
- Each pillar broad enough for ongoing content but specific enough for audience relevance
- Pillars should collectively cover the full customer journey
- Examples: Educational, Behind-the-Scenes, Community, Thought Leadership, Product

### The 70/20/10 Rule
- 70% proven content types (what already works)
- 20% experimental content (new formats, new angles)
- 10% reactive content (trend-jacking, real-time marketing)

### The Hub-and-Spoke Model
- Hub: Long-form cornerstone content (guides, research, reports)
- Spokes: Derivative content pieces distributed across platforms
- Each spoke links back to or references the hub content
- Maximizes content ROI through strategic repurposing

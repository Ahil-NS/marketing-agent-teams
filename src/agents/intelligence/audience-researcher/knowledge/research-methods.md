# Audience Research Methods

## TAM/SAM/SOM Audience Sizing

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)
- Estimate the total number of people who could potentially use the product
- Use industry reports, census data, and market research for sizing
- Include all geographies and demographics that overlap with the product category

### Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
- Narrow TAM to the audience reachable through the brand's current channels
- Factor in geographic constraints, language, and platform availability
- Estimate based on platform user bases in target regions

### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
- Realistic segment the brand can capture in the near term
- Based on current brand awareness, marketing budget, and competitive positioning
- Typically 1-10% of SAM for new entrants, higher for established brands

## Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Audience Analysis

### Functional Jobs
- What task is the audience trying to accomplish?
- What tools or solutions do they currently use?
- Where do current solutions fall short?

### Emotional Jobs
- How does the audience want to feel when using a product in this category?
- What frustrations or anxieties does the current state create?
- What aspirational identity does the audience want to project?

### Social Jobs
- How does the audience want to be perceived by others?
- What social validation do they seek from this product category?
- How does the product fit into their social identity?

### JTBD Application to Content
- Map content topics to functional jobs (educational content solving real problems)
- Map content tone to emotional jobs (empathetic, empowering, aspirational)
- Map content format to social jobs (shareable, identity-signaling, community-building)

## Psychographic Segmentation (VALS Framework)

### VALS Types Relevant to Marketing
- **Innovators**: High-resource, high-motivation. Open to new ideas, early adopters. Respond to cutting-edge content.
- **Thinkers**: Educated, reflective. Value information and substance. Respond to data-driven, long-form content.
- **Achievers**: Goal-oriented, brand-conscious. Value success signals. Respond to aspirational, results-focused content.
- **Experiencers**: Young, enthusiastic, impulsive. Value excitement and variety. Respond to bold visual content, trends.
- **Believers**: Conservative, conventional. Value tradition and community. Respond to authentic, trust-building content.
- **Strivers**: Trendy, fun-loving but resource-constrained. Value social approval. Respond to aspirational but accessible content.
- **Makers**: Practical, self-sufficient. Value functionality. Respond to how-to content and practical demonstrations.
- **Survivors**: Cautious, risk-averse. Value safety and familiarity. Respond to reassurance and testimonial content.

### Applying VALS to Content Strategy
- Map each audience segment to a primary VALS type
- Tailor content tone, format, and messaging to match VALS motivations
- Use VALS alignment to predict content format preferences

## Behavioral Segmentation

### Usage Behavior
- Heavy vs. light users of the product category
- Frequency of platform usage (daily active vs. weekly browser)
- Content consumption patterns (scroll, engage, create, lurk)

### Purchase Behavior
- Research-heavy vs. impulse buyers
- Price sensitivity and value orientation
- Brand loyalty vs. deal-seeking behavior

### Engagement Behavior
- Active engagers (comment, share, create) vs. passive consumers (scroll, read)
- Community contributors vs. lurkers
- Cross-platform behavior (same content on multiple platforms vs. platform-specific)

## Platform-Specific Audience Research

### Reddit: Subreddit Analysis
- Identify relevant subreddits using Reddit search and crosspost patterns
- Analyze subscriber counts, daily active discussions, and post engagement ratios
- Study sidebar rules and community culture to understand audience norms
- Map subreddit overlap: which other subreddits do these users frequent?
- Identify key contributors and thought leaders in target subreddits
- Note typical content formats that succeed (text posts, AMAs, data visualizations)

### TikTok: Creator Audience Overlap
- Identify creators in the brand's niche and analyze their audience demographics
- Study comment patterns on niche content to understand audience language and priorities
- Analyze trending sounds and hashtags in the niche to map interest clusters
- Look for "stitching" and "dueting" patterns that reveal audience content preferences
- Note audience age skew and content velocity expectations

### Facebook: Audience Insights Proxy
- Research Facebook group membership and activity levels in product category
- Analyze Page engagement metrics for competitors and adjacent brands
- Map age, gender, and geographic distributions common in the product niche
- Study Facebook Marketplace activity for purchase intent signals
- Note group interaction patterns: question-asking, advice-seeking, sharing

### Instagram: Hashtag Audience Mapping
- Research hashtag volumes and engagement rates for product-relevant tags
- Analyze follower demographics of competitor and adjacent brand accounts
- Map visual aesthetic preferences in the niche (polished vs. raw vs. minimalist)
- Study Reels engagement patterns vs. static posts vs. Stories
- Identify micro-influencers whose audiences overlap with target segments

## Demographic Research

- Age distribution using platform analytics and industry reports
- Gender split with consideration for non-binary and diverse audiences
- Geographic distribution: urban vs. suburban vs. rural, key metro areas
- Income and education levels from census and industry data
- Professional background and industry vertical
- Life stage: students, early career, parents, retirees, etc.

## Community Mapping

- Identify relevant subreddits, Facebook groups, Discord servers, and TikTok niches
- Estimate community size (subscriber/member count) and engagement rate (posts/comments per day)
- Note community culture and norms (formal vs. casual, promotional tolerance)
- Track influential voices and thought leaders within each community
- Map community cross-pollination: where do members of one community also participate?

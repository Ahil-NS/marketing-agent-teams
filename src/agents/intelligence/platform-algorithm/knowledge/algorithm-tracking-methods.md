# Algorithm Tracking Methods

## How to Research Algorithm Changes

Platform algorithms change frequently — sometimes weekly. Staying current is
essential for providing accurate optimization advice. This guide outlines the
most reliable methods for tracking algorithm changes.

## Primary Sources (Most Reliable)

### Official Platform Blogs
- **Reddit:** reddit.com/r/changelog, reddit.com/r/announcements
- **TikTok:** newsroom.tiktok.com, TikTok Creator Portal
- **Instagram:** about.instagram.com/blog, Instagram @creators account
- **Facebook:** about.fb.com/news, engineering.fb.com

### Platform Engineering Blogs
- Detail technical changes to ranking systems
- Often include explanations of why changes were made
- Published weeks/months after the change is implemented
- Most authoritative source for algorithm mechanics

### Platform Creator Documentation
- TikTok Creative Center (trends, effects, best practices)
- Instagram Professional Dashboard insights
- Facebook Creator Studio documentation
- Reddit's wiki on content policies and algorithm mechanics

## Secondary Sources (Industry Analysis)

### Creator Economy Newsletters
- **Social Media Today** — Daily updates on platform changes
- **Later.com Blog** — Platform-specific algorithm analysis
- **Hootsuite Blog** — Cross-platform social media strategy
- **Buffer Blog** — Social media research and insights
- **Creator Economy Newsletter** — Business of content creation

### Platform-Specific Creators Who Track Changes
- Many creator-educators track and report algorithm changes
- Search "[platform] algorithm update [current month]" for recent analysis
- YouTube has active "algorithm explained" creators who often cover other platforms too
- Reddit has subreddits dedicated to discussing algorithm behavior (r/NewToReddit, r/TikTok, etc.)

### Industry Research Reports
- Sprout Social — Annual social media reports with engagement data
- HubSpot — Marketing industry research and benchmarks
- Socialbakers (Emplifi) — Platform benchmark reports
- Rival IQ — Social media benchmark reports

## Research Strategies

### Detecting Algorithm Changes
1. **Engagement pattern shifts** — Sudden changes in typical engagement rates
   across many creators often indicate algorithm updates
2. **Creator community discussions** — Check Reddit, Twitter, and TikTok for
   creators discussing sudden changes in their reach/engagement
3. **Platform announcements** — Check official blogs and creator accounts
4. **A/B test observations** — Platforms often A/B test changes before full rollout;
   watch for user reports of different experiences
5. **Content type performance shifts** — If one format suddenly performs better/worse,
   the algorithm likely changed its format weighting

### Verifying Algorithm Claims
1. Cross-reference claims with multiple sources (min 2-3)
2. Check the date — algorithm information older than 90 days may be outdated
3. Distinguish between confirmed changes and speculation
4. Look for data-backed analysis, not just anecdotal reports
5. Consider the source's credibility and potential biases

### Building a Change Timeline
1. Document each confirmed change with date, platform, and impact
2. Track the source of the information for credibility assessment
3. Note whether the change is a test (limited rollout) or full deployment
4. Rate the significance (minor tweak vs. major ranking change)
5. Assess the half-life of the information (some changes are temporary)

## Web Search Strategies for Algorithm Research

### Effective Search Queries
- "[platform] algorithm update [month] [year]"
- "[platform] ranking changes [current year]"
- "[platform] content distribution update"
- "[platform] creator reach decline/increase [month]"
- "[platform] engineering blog algorithm"
- "social media algorithm changes [current month]"

### Search Filtering
- Filter by date: Last month or last 3 months for current information
- Look for authoritative domains (platform official sites, established publications)
- Cross-reference findings across multiple search results
- Check the publication date of every source — treat undated sources as suspect

## Evaluation Framework

When assessing algorithm information, score it on these dimensions:

### Recency (Critical)
- < 30 days: Very current (high confidence)
- 30-90 days: Current (moderate confidence)
- 90-180 days: Possibly outdated (low confidence)
- > 180 days: Likely outdated (verify before using)

### Source Authority
- Official platform documentation: Highest authority
- Platform engineering blog: Very high authority
- Industry research firm: High authority
- Established industry publication: Moderate-high authority
- Individual creator report: Moderate authority (verify with other sources)
- Forum discussion: Low authority (treat as hypothesis)

### Signal Strength
- Multiple independent sources confirm: Strong signal
- Single authoritative source: Moderate signal
- Anecdotal reports only: Weak signal
- Contradictory reports: Uncertain (investigate further)
